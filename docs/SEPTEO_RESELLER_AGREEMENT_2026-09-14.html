Founders1 GmbH (“Founders1”) operates BetterCo, the platform through which it procures company registry documents and structured registry data from official registers and other procurement routes, holds them, and delivers them with provenance. Throughout this Agreement, Founders1 is the contracting party and BetterCo is the service STP connects to. STP supplies software and information services to legal, notarial and tax practices and wishes to offer that service to its clients in its own name. Annex 1 describes the service; this Agreement sets the terms on which STP resells it.

Annex 1 to the Reseller Agreement

Service description
and reseller use

Founders1 GmbH — STP Business Information GmbH · Draft, 14 September 2026

1.1 What the service provides

Founders1 supplies company registry documents and registry data for the jurisdictions listed in Annex 2, through BetterCo. Per company — one case — Founders1 procures and delivers:

  • the registry extract, the articles of association and, where the register evidences it, the shareholder record, together with the structured registry data;
  • for every document: the source reference at the register, the retrieval date and, where the document carries one, the content date, the document version, the evidence level, and — where the document allows it — a machine-readable text layer, since registry documents are frequently scans without one;
  • an explicit answer where a document does not exist.

1.2 Evidence level

Not every document that carries an item of information is also evidence of it. Each delivery therefore states an evidence level:

LevelMeaningExample
dataan item of information, not a document — never evidence in itselfstructured shareholder particulars
documenta document actually filed with the register, without a sealarticles of association from the register
certifiedissued and sealed by the registry authorityKbis, certified commercial register extract

STP does not have to specify the level when ordering. Where more than one level is obtainable for a document kind in a jurisdiction, Founders1 delivers the highest obtainable level and labels it. An apostille is a property of a certified document, not a fourth level.

1.3 Availability

Availability is classified per jurisdiction × legal form × document kind. Exactly one of four cases applies, and the fourth is a definitive answer rather than an error:

CaseMeaningPrice effect
Base documentpart of the standard retrievalincluded in the base price
Additional documentheld by the register, not part of the standard retrievalsurcharge per Annex 2
Data onlyno document, but structured particulars from the registerincluded in the base price
Not availablenot obtainable in this jurisdiction for this legal formno charge

One point of supply

STP reads exclusively from BetterCo. The procurement routes behind it are not visible or addressable — not as an endpoint, a field name, or a status value.

BetterCo is the system of record: documents are held, versioned and re-served there. When Founders1 adds or changes a procurement route, STP sees it in cost and provenance — not in code.

1.4 How STP uses the service with its clients

STP integrates the service into its own onboarding, KYC and client-file workflows and offers it to its clients under its own name. Founders1 does not appear towards the end client and has no contractual relationship with them. Per end-client company:

  1. STP opens a case: jurisdiction, legal form, company identifiers and the document kinds required.
  2. Procurement runs asynchronously. Some routes take minutes; manual registry routes take hours to days. Progress is reported by callback, and each document kind carries its own state:
    • requested — the document kind has been asked for
    • ordered — procurement has been triggered
    • delivered — the document has arrived at BetterCo
    • stored — it is held and retrievable by STP
    • failed or not available — the two end states that carry no document
  3. STP retrieves the documents and their provenance from BetterCo and files them in the client's file. Case and document identifiers are BetterCo identifiers and stay stable across a change of source.
  4. Where a document of the required kind is already held for that company and still current, it is re-served without a new procurement and without a new charge. Founders1 determines the currency criteria per jurisdiction and document kind and states them in Annex 2a.
  5. Where the register provides it, each document carries two dates: the retrieval date and the content date. They are often not the same — a shareholding statement retrieved today may reproduce a much earlier filing. Founders1 delivers both wherever the documents allow, and suggests that STP's own currency rules build on the content date where its process permits.

Data protection between

Founders1 GmbH — STP Business Information GmbH · Draft, 14 September 2026

3.1 Roles

Each party determines the purposes and means of its own processing and acts as an independent controller under Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR. Neither party processes on behalf of the other; Art. 28 GDPR does not apply, and no joint controllership under Art. 26 GDPR is established. Each party is separately responsible for the lawfulness of its own processing.

Founders1 decides which route a Case takes, what is retrieved, and how long it is held, and is the controller of that processing along its whole procurement chain; parties Founders1 engages for procurement act as its processors. Delivery to STP is therefore a transfer between two controllers, not a disclosure by a processor.

3.2 What is processed

Data subjectsCategories of data
Directors and officersname, function, date of birth, address as held by the register
Shareholdersname, address, shareholding, capital contribution as held by the register
Beneficial ownersname, date of birth, nationality, nature and extent of the interest
Users at STPname, business contact details, account and log data of the API

No special categories under Art. 9 GDPR are processed. The register content originates from public company registers; Founders1 does not obtain it from the data subject.

3.3 Purposes and legal basis

Processing serves the procurement, delivery and documentation of registry information so that STP and its End Clients can meet their obligations under money-laundering law. Each party relies on its own legal basis, as a rule Art. 6 (1) (b) or (f) GDPR, and for End Clients subject to money-laundering law additionally Art. 6 (1) (c) GDPR.

The information duty under Art. 14 GDPR falls on each party for its own processing. Each party assesses independently whether Art. 14 (5) GDPR applies.

3.4 Technical and organisational measures

  • Encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest for stored documents; access only over authenticated, rate-limited API access bound to STP's account.
  • Role-based access within Founders1, restricted to staff who need it; access to production data logged.
  • Documents and case records are held within the EU/EEA. Founders1 names the hosting location and its sub-processors on request and notifies STP before a change.
  • Segregation of test and production environments; backups encrypted; restore tested.
  • Retention and deletion follow Annex 1 and clause 14.4.

3.5 Cooperation

Each party informs the other without undue delay of a personal data breach affecting data received from the other, with the information needed for that party's own notification duties. Data-subject requests are answered by the party that received them; where a request concerns the other party's processing, it is forwarded without undue delay. Each party names a contact point for data protection matters on signature.

This Annex obliges STP. It grants Founders1 no rights against an End Client and creates no contract between them. Where STP's existing terms already cover a point in substance, that point is satisfied.

Where a Document carries a register-specific restriction notified with it under section 5.2 (e), that restriction prevails over anything more permissive in STP's own terms.

5.2 The terms

  • (a) Purpose. The End Client may use the documents and registry data delivered for it for its own know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering checks, client and supplier onboarding, advisory work, and its own record-keeping. This includes documents about the End Client's own customers, counterparties and suppliers, obtained to discharge the End Client's own obligations. It does not cover discharging another person's compliance obligations, or providing the documents to another person for that purpose.
  • (b) Retention. The End Client may retain what was delivered for it for as long as its own statutory retention obligation requires, including after its contract with STP ends and after the Agreement between STP and Founders1 ends. Beyond that period, and for any use outside the purpose in (a), the rights follow what Founders1 holds in the underlying content; the applicable scope is stated with each Document.
  • (c) No redistribution. The documents are for the End Client's own file. The End Client does not pass them on to third parties, does not sell or license the structured data as a dataset, does not publish it, and does not extract it in bulk. Three disclosures are permitted: to its own advisers and auditors bound to confidentiality; to a supervisory authority where required; and to the company a document concerns, in connection with the End Client's own check on that company.
  • (d) No competing use. The End Client does not use the documents, the registry data or the availability information to build, train, operate or supply a company-data, registry-aggregation or document-procurement product or service for third parties.
  • (e) Register restrictions. Some registers restrict what may be done with their content. Where such a restriction is notified with a document, it applies to the End Client and prevails over the permissions above.
  • (f) The register is the source. Registry content is reproduced as it was filed or held. Neither STP nor its supplier warrants that the register itself is accurate, complete or up to date, and a document reproduces the state of the register at the retrieval date stated with it.
  • (g) Data protection. The documents contain personal data of directors, shareholders and beneficial owners. The End Client is an independent controller for its own use of them and is responsible for the lawfulness of that use, for its own information duties, and for its own assessment of Art. 14 (5) GDPR.
  • (h) No further rights. The End Client acquires no rights in the platform, the interface, the coverage and routing data, or the jurisdiction and document-kind classification behind the service.

5.3 Changes

Founders1 may amend this Annex where a register, a supplier or applicable law requires it, on 30 days' notice, evidencing the requirement. STP applies the change to End Client terms concluded or renewed after it takes effect; it does not have to reopen terms already agreed.

ADDED 14.09. — the chain reaches a bank, and what that changed
The real chain is Founders1 → Septeo → a large bank → that bank's own customers. The bank uses the Documents to evaluate its own customers — so the bank's customers are the subjects of the Documents, not recipients of the service. That is the narrow case, and it needs no fourth tier: the bank is an End Client like any other, and nothing is passed on. Four adjustments were made, all conservative, none widening the grant: (1) Annex 5(a) now says in terms that own-use covers Documents about the End Client's customers, counterparties and suppliers — previously only implied, and a bank's counsel would have asked. (2) Annex 5(c) permitted only advisers and auditors; it now also permits disclosure to a supervisory authority, and to the company a Document concerns. Without the latter, a bank showing a corporate customer what it holds on it would breach its own terms — routine in practice, and the old wording forbade it. (3) Clause 3.4 now names regulated institutions as professional End Clients, rather than leaving it to inference, and states expressly that onward provision as a service is not in the field of use. (4) New clause 9.5, below. Not done, deliberately: no sub-licence to the bank's customers, no recursive pass-through, no chain cap. Those belong to the other case — a bank reselling or bundling the service — which is not what is happening and should not be drafted for speculatively. If that changes, it is a real amendment, not a tweak.
ADDED 14.09. — clause 9.5, the protective clause
A bank brings an apparatus that a Kanzlei does not: DORA (Art. 28 ff. VO (EU) 2022/2554), outsourcing requirements, ISMS, register of information, audit rights, exit assistance. The KfW briefing in the marketing repo records exactly this shape — „Prüfung nach Art. 28 Abs. 4 … vor Zuschlag“ and „Die Risiken liegen nicht in der Funktion, sondern im Bankbetrieb“. Septeo will be asked for all of it and could otherwise commit us to it without our knowing. 9.5 puts the obligation on STP and bars it from agreeing audit rights, route or sub-supplier disclosure, exit assistance, incident-reporting periods or register entries on our behalf without our written agreement — softened by a good-faith undertaking not to refuse unreasonably what the End Client's supervisory position genuinely requires, since a flat refusal would simply lose the deal. The collision to watch: DORA subcontractor transparency runs against clause 6.1, which makes procurement routes invisible and unaddressable, and which the internal note says has never been breached towards Septeo. 9.5 takes the defensible line — Founders1 is the supplier of record, its procurement parties are its own sub-suppliers — the same position Annex 3.1 already takes for data protection, and consistent with the upstream contract's controller/processor split. Expect it to be tested. Decide in advance how far route disclosure to a bank's compliance function under NDA can go before it is worth losing the deal over.

WHY RESELLER STATUS IS WORTH DEFENDING — it is the DORA shield
Reseller status and § 89b protection point the same way, so the fixes below serve both. As a reseller, STP is the bank's ICT third-party service provider and Founders1 is STP's subcontractor. The Art. 30 contractual apparatus lands on STP, and only what STP passes down reaches us. If the relationship were read as agency — STP selling for Founders1's account — then Founders1 would be supplying the bank, and the bank's contractual requirements, audit rights and register entry would attach to us directly. That is the difference the 50 % share puts at risk, and it is a stronger reason to defend the characterisation than the Ausgleichsanspruch is. A phrase in 9.5 was working against this and has been corrected. It read „Towards the End Client, Founders1 is the supplier of record“ — intended to mean do not look behind Founders1 at the procurement chain, but on a plain reading it says Founders1 supplies the bank, which is the agency position and the opposite of what is wanted. It now states that STP is the supplier towards the End Client and Founders1 is STP's own supplier. What reseller status does not do: it does not make us invisible. DORA follows the functional ICT supply chain, not the commercial label. Art. 28(3) requires the bank's register of information to cover subcontractors, and where the service underpins a critical or important function parts of the Art. 30(3) catalogue — incident notification, exit support, audit access — genuinely have to reach the subcontractor. Refusing outright would simply end the deal. New 9.6 therefore defines the cooperation package on our terms rather than leaving Septeo to agree one on ours: documentation and certifications first, audits exercised through STP and satisfied by a report or a pooled audit where possible, on-site audits limited to the service and expressly not extending to procurement-route identity (clause 6.1), and chargeable beyond the standard documentation. To settle before a bank is named: whether the service will be treated as supporting a critical or important function at that bank. If yes, expect the full Art. 30(3) list and price the effort in; if no, 9.6 is likely sufficient as drafted.
CHECK — are we reselling at all? The 50 % share cuts against it
A profit share rather than a purchase price is the classic indicator that an arrangement is not a resale. It matters for three reasons, and the exposure is real but manageable. (1) § 89b HGB Ausgleichsanspruch. The BGH applies it by analogy to a Vertragshändler who is integrated into the supplier's sales organisation and is obliged to hand over its customer base, so that the supplier can exploit it after termination. A claim can reach a year's average commission and cannot be waived in advance once the relationship qualifies. We had the second prong half-open: clause 8.5 required per-Case reporting of the amount invoiced to the End Client, and clause 6.2 assumes we learn End Client identities — ongoing transmission during the term can satisfy the customer-base prong even without a transfer obligation. Fixed: 8.5 now lets STP report by its own reference instead of by name and records that we do not require identities for settlement; new 15.5 states that STP is not obliged to transfer its End Client base and that anything transmitted under 8.5 is for settlement and audit only. (2) The characterisation itself. The strong reseller indicators are already present and should stay: STP sets its own prices (8.1), invoices in its own name, carries the relationship, and — because „Gross Revenue“ is net only of credits and refunds, not of bad debt — bears the credit risk on its End Clients. Do not concede that last point in negotiation: the moment our share is reduced by an End Client's non-payment, STP stops bearing commercial risk and the agency reading gets much stronger. Clause 3.1 said only „in its own name“ where 2.1 said „in its own name and for its own account“; the grant clause now carries the full formula. New 15.5 adds the express no-agency, no-Kommission, no-partnership statement, which the Agreement previously lacked entirely. (3) Competition law, and why 8.1 earns its place. Because STP sets the end price, we avoid resale price maintenance — a hardcore restriction under Art. 101 TFEU and the VBER. The mirror image is that a genuine agency falls outside Art. 101(1) altogether, so if we ever wanted to control end pricing we would have to accept agency status and the § 89b exposure with it. Pick one; we have picked reseller. What this does not fix: the upstream blocker is indifferent to the label. Their clause 10.2 bars use „for the benefit of any other entity or person“, not merely re-sale, so recharacterising the Septeo relationship does not escape it. Still needs the upstream addendum. Worth a German lawyer's eye before signature — this is a characterisation question, and what is set out here is structural analysis, not advice.
